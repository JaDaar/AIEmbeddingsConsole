Bread rises because yeast ferments sugars in the dough, producing carbon dioxide gas. The gluten network formed by kneading traps the gas, causing the dough to expand. A warm, humid environment speeds up fermentation.

To make a basic vinaigrette, whisk three parts oil with one part vinegar, then season with salt, pepper and a little mustard. The mustard acts as an emulsifier, helping the oil and vinegar stay mixed.

Searing meat at high temperature triggers the Maillard reaction, a chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars that creates browned colour and complex savoury flavours.

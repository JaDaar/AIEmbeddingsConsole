Tomato plants need six to eight hours of direct sunlight a day and steady, deep watering. Uneven watering causes the fruit to crack or develop blossom-end rot, a dark sunken patch caused by a calcium shortage in the growing fruit. Pinching off the small shoots that form between the main stem and the branches, called suckers, directs the plant's energy into fruit instead of leaves.

Compost improves almost any garden soil. It loosens heavy clay, helps sandy soil hold water, and feeds the microorganisms that make nutrients available to plant roots. A good compost pile mixes green material such as vegetable scraps and grass clippings with brown material such as dry leaves and cardboard, and is turned every week or two to let air in.
